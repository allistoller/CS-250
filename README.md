# CS-250: Software Development Lifecycle

## Sprint Review and Retrospective

This repository includes my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS-250: Software Development Lifecycle at Southern New Hampshire University. Throughout this course, I explored Agile and Scrum practices by participating in different roles within the SNHU Travel project, including Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developer, and Tester. These experiences helped me better understand how software development teams collaborate, adapt to changes, and deliver products that meet customer needs.

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

Understanding user needs starts with identifying what the customer wants to accomplish and how the software can help them achieve that goal. User stories help translate those needs into smaller, manageable requirements that developers can work toward. During the SNHU Travel project, I practiced creating user stories from the customer's perspective, which helped me understand the importance of focusing on functionality that provides value to the user. I learned that clear requirements and communication between the Product Owner, developers, and testers are important for making sure the final product meets expectations.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing programs, I think it is important to start by understanding the requirements, breaking the project into manageable tasks, and testing functionality throughout development. This course showed me how Agile allows teams to work in smaller increments rather than waiting until the end of a project to evaluate their progress. In my future development work, I hope to incorporate practices such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Reviews, and Retrospectives. I especially appreciate how Agile encourages continuous improvement and allows teams to adjust when requirements change.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating effectively, being dependable, and supporting others while working toward a shared goal. Throughout this course, I learned that each Scrum role contributes to the success of a project and that collaboration is just as important as technical skills. A good team member should be open to feedback, willing to help resolve challenges, and able to adapt when priorities change. I believe that maintaining a positive attitude, taking responsibility for assigned work, and communicating openly can make a significant difference in the success of a development team.
